Habit taper calculator / Video games

Plan a gradual video game taper.

You like games and you’d like to play a bit less of them. Slope schedules game-free days and spreads them a little further apart each phase, so the change comes in steps you can plan around.

The calculator loads a 2:1 ratio over 30 days: two game-free days, then one day you play as you normally would, working up to five game-free days in a row by the last week. A month shows you which evenings were really about the game and which were about something else. If you play most nights for hours, give it 60 days instead, or start at 1:1.

What counts as an off day

Gaming is a hobby, and a good one. This page isn’t here to argue otherwise. It’s for adults whose play has crept past where they meant it to be: the session that was going to be an hour, the weeknight that ended at 2 a.m., the other things you keep meaning to get to. The World Health Organization makes the same distinction: it notes that only a small share of people who play have a gaming problem, and suggests that everyone who plays keep an eye on how much time it takes and what it pushes out.

A game-free day works because it has no fine print. Either you played or you didn’t. Settle the edges before day one:

  • Every platform counts. Console, PC, handheld, and the phone. If the plan covers the PlayStation but not the mobile game you open in bed, the hours just move.
  • Watching is your call. Streams, esports and let’s-plays can stay allowed or go on the list. Decide once, write it down, and don’t renegotiate it at 10 p.m.
  • Maintenance counts as play. Collecting daily login rewards, tending a farm, checking the auction house. These are designed to take two minutes and turn into forty.

Mogul days are ordinary gaming days. Play what you like. One adjustment that helps: give each mogul day a stop time. Not a session length, which stretches easily, but a clock time, like “controller down at 11.” As the phases go on, you may find the mogul days start earlier and end sooner without being told to.

“One more match”

Most games are built around loops that end on a cliff: the next round starts in ten seconds, the queue is already searching, you’re one level from an unlock, the raid resets tomorrow. None of that is a character flaw on your part. It’s design, and it’s very good at its job. The effort of an off day mostly lands in one place: the stretch after dinner when the evening opens up and nothing else has the same pull. That’s the hour to plan for.

It also shows up at the other end. A review of studies on adult players found that heavy gaming was linked to later bedtimes, shorter sleep and more insomnia. If the thing you really want back is sleep, the stop time on mogul days may matter as much as the off days themselves.

Cues and what to put in their place

Games attach to routines: the couch after work, the friend who messages “on?”, the Sunday afternoon with nothing booked, the laptop that doubles as the gaming rig. Some of these you can change for a few weeks:

  • Add a step. Unplug the console and put the controller in a drawer, log out of the launcher, or move the handheld to another room. A thirty-second delay is enough to turn a reflex into a choice.
  • Tell your group. If you play with friends, share your off days. “I’m out Tuesday and Wednesday, back Thursday” lands better than disappearing, and it removes the invitation that’s hardest to turn down.
  • Fill the evening before it starts. Off days go better with a plan for the first hour: cooking something slow, a walk, a class, calling someone, a book you’re halfway through. Something with its own momentum works better than “I’ll find something.”
  • Keep the parts you value. If what you miss is the social side, a voice call with the group on an off day is still time with them.

Detours

Playing on an off day is a detour. Log it and carry on with the plan as written; don’t cancel your next mogul day to even things out. A quick match that ended when you said it would is worth noting differently from one that ran past midnight, and the log will show you which kind you tend to have. If you detour more than twice in one phase, use the app’s extend option and stay at that ratio longer. If the detours keep landing on the same night, look at what that night has in it. It’s often a friend group’s regular session, and that’s a scheduling problem more than a willpower one: make that night a mogul day.

Who this is for

Adults who enjoy games and want them to take up less of the week, without selling the console or swearing off the hobby. If gaming has become a way to get through anxiety or depression, or it’s costing you work, sleep or relationships and cutting back hasn’t worked, a therapist or your doctor is a better first stop.
