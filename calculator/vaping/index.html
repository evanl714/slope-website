Habit taper calculator / Vaping

Plan a gradual vaping taper.

Vaping is an all-day habit, which makes “cut back” hard to measure. Slope turns it into something you can see: vape-free days, spaced a little further apart each phase.

The calculator starts you at a 1:2 ratio over 60 days, which means one vape-free day for every two regular days, building to six vape-free days in a row by your finish date. Change either setting to fit where you actually are.

Read this first. For cigarettes, the best evidence shows that quitting abruptly with support works at least as well as reducing first, and sometimes better. Nobody has shown the opposite for vaping. If you can set a quit date and stop, do that. A taper is for people who’ve tried that and want a more gradual on-ramp, and it works better with support: nicotine replacement, a quitline, or a clinician. Details in tapering vs. cold turkey.

How to use this schedule for vaping

Puff counts drift and nobody keeps them honestly. A vape-free day is a clean yes-or-no, and that’s what Slope schedules. Decide what an off day means before you start:

  • Device stays home. The simplest version. If it isn’t in your pocket, most of the day’s puffs never happen.
  • Device stays off. Charged, in a drawer, not touched. Harder, and a better rehearsal for the end of the plan.
  • Nicotine-free day. Off days are zero-nicotine, but you can use a 0 mg liquid if the hand-to-mouth habit is the hard part. Some people find this keeps the habit alive; others find it’s the only way through week one. Try it and be honest about which you are.

On mogul days, vape as you normally would, with one change: step the nicotine strength down as the phases progress. Moving from a 50 mg salt to a 25, then a 10, over the course of the plan means each off day starts from a lower baseline, and the withdrawal on those days is milder.

What withdrawal looks like on off days

Nicotine withdrawal is not medically dangerous, but it is unpleasant and it peaks early. On your first off days expect irritability, restlessness, trouble concentrating, a foggy afternoon, and cravings that arrive in short, sharp waves. Most waves pass in a few minutes. The Slope app’s Ride It Out exercise exists for exactly that stretch.

Withdrawal is worst in the first week of any change and mostly fades over two to four weeks. On a spaced schedule that means the early phases carry the hard days, and by the time you reach 3:1 or 4:1, an off day is mostly about habit and cues rather than chemistry. That’s the point of doing it this way.

Cues, not just cravings

Vaping attaches to moments: the walk to the car, the end of a meal, the first minute of a break, a specific friend. Off days show you which cues are strongest, because those are the ones you’ll notice reaching for. Two things help:

  • Replace the motion. Gum, a toothpick, a cold drink, a water bottle with a straw. It sounds trivial and it consistently works.
  • Plan the cue. If the after-lunch puff is the one you can’t skip, look at the calendar and see which off days have a lunch you can control: eat with someone who doesn’t vape, or take the walk in a different direction.

Detours and stalls

A puff on an off day is a detour. Log it, keep the next mogul day where it is, and don’t add punishment days. If you’re detouring more than twice per phase, stay in that phase longer using the app’s extend option rather than moving on. And if the whole plan stalls at 2:1 for weeks, that’s useful information: it may be time to add nicotine replacement or talk to someone who does this for a living. Free quitlines exist in most countries and they’re good at this.

Who this is for

Adults who vape and want a structured way to cut down or stop. If you’re under 18, pregnant, or vaping alongside other substances, talk to a clinician before making a plan. Slope is a habit-planning tool, not medical advice or a cessation treatment.
