Habit taper calculator / Chewing tobacco

Plan a gradual chewing tobacco taper.

Dip tends to ride along with everything else you do: the drive, the job site, the ballgame, the hour after dinner. Slope gives you specific days when the can stays shut, and spaces the dip days further apart each phase.

The default plan is built for someone who dips or chews every day. It lasts 60 days and opens at 1:2: two dip days, then one dip-free day, repeated for the first nine days. After that the gap widens in steps, from every other day to two, three, four and five dip-free days between each dip day. Across the whole plan you have 19 dip days and 41 without, and the last four days are all dip-free. If you only dip on weekends or at work, shorten it or start at a wider ratio.

Read this first. Tapering has not been tested against stopping outright for dip, snuff or chew. The research that does exist is on cigarettes, and there a Cochrane review found neither route led to more people quitting long term. So a gradual plan is not the better method, just a different one. If you can pick a day in the next couple of weeks and stop, that is a good plan too. Either way, you do better with help. Smokefree says nicotine replacement can double your chances of quitting smokeless tobacco for good, usually a patch plus gum or lozenges. A quitline gives free coaching over the phone (in the US, 1-800-QUIT-NOW), and a doctor, dentist or pharmacist can help you choose. More on the trade-off in tapering vs. cold turkey.

What a dip-free day means

Counting pinches or cans is how most people try to cut back, and it rarely holds, because one more pinch never feels like a decision. Slope skips the counting on off days. An off day has one rule: nothing with tobacco or nicotine goes in your mouth or nose. That covers every form the FDA groups as smokeless tobacco: loose leaf, plug and twist, moist snuff, snus and dry snuff. It also covers nicotine pouches. Switching to pouches on an off day moves the habit to a different can rather than giving you a day without it.

Nicotine replacement is the exception. If you use a patch, gum or lozenge as part of this plan, it doesn’t count against an off day. Ask a pharmacist how to dose it for the amount you dip now.

On dip days, pick your number in the morning. If a can usually lasts you a day and a half, make it last two, then three as the phases move on. A mogul day with a ceiling you chose ahead of time does more for the plan than one where you just try to go easy.

What an off day asks of you

People who dip reach blood nicotine levels similar to people who smoke, and the National Cancer Institute notes it stays in the blood longer. So the first off days are about chemistry as much as habit. Smokefree lists what to expect: poor sleep, a short temper, anxiety or restlessness, a low mood, and trouble thinking clearly. For most people the worst of it lasts a few days to a few weeks.

Cravings come as brief, strong urges, and each one passes. The effort on an off day is getting through those few minutes a handful of times, often at the exact moments you used to reach for the can. That is what the app’s Ride It Out exercise is for. Because the default plan front-loads the dip days, you meet the hardest off days one at a time, with a dip day on either side, instead of all at once.

Where dip lives, and what to put there instead

Dip is portable and needs no lighter, no break and no smoking area, so it spreads into places cigarettes can’t reach: long drives, shifts on a crew, a tree stand, a fishing boat, the dugout. On your first off days, notice where your hand goes to your back pocket. Those are your cues.

  • Give your mouth a job. Sugar-free gum, sunflower seeds in the shell, a toothpick, a cinnamon stick or a cold drink. Something to hold in your cheek or work with your teeth covers a lot of what the pinch was doing.
  • Move the can. A can in the truck, one in your jacket and one in the desk means three decisions every hour. On off days, leave every can at home. If you want it gone completely, wait until the final phase to throw it out.
  • Change the first ten minutes. If the first dip happens as you pull out of the driveway, put a pack of gum in the cupholder the night before an off day, or take a different route.
  • Tell the people you dip with. A crew or a team that shares cans will offer one. Saying “not today” is easier when they already know the plan.

Detours

A dip on an off day is a detour. Spit it out, log it, and keep the next dip day where the calendar has it. Don’t add a make-up day. If detours keep landing on the same cue, fix the cue: move the can, plan the substitute, or trade the off day with a mogul day that doesn’t include that drive or that game. Two or more detours in a phase means you should extend the phase in the app instead of moving on. If the plan stalls for a few weeks, add nicotine replacement or call a quitline. Free coaching exists for dip too.

The plan is worth sticking with. Smokefree notes that after you stop, mouth sores start to heal, taste and smell come back, and gum loss slows.

Who this is for

Adults who dip, chew or use snuff and want a structured way to cut down on the way to stopping. If you’re under 18 or pregnant, or you use other substances too, talk to a clinician before you start. If you have a sore or a white or red patch in your mouth that isn’t healing, have a dentist or doctor look at it whatever plan you choose. Slope is a habit-planning tool, not medical advice or treatment.
