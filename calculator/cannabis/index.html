Habit taper calculator / Cannabis

Plan a gradual weed taper.

When you smoke every day, a single day without it can feel like a big ask. Slope starts small and puts a little more room between smoking days with each phase.

This page is set up for daily users. The calculator opens at a 1:2 ratio over 90 days: two days when you can use as you do now, then one THC-free day, on repeat. Every twelve to fifteen days the ratio moves. First one on, one off. Then two off days per use day, then three, and so on, until you reach six THC-free days in a row near the end. Across the three months that works out to 62 off days and 28 mogul days. If you already take a day or two off each week, start further along.

Read this first. For most people, cannabis withdrawal is uncomfortable but not dangerous. A 2022 clinical review notes that it does not carry a high risk of severe outcomes. Talk to a clinician before you start, or at any point along the way, if you use alcohol or other substances alongside cannabis, if you are pregnant, or if you find you can’t cut down however you set the plan. In the US, SAMHSA’s National Helpline is free, confidential and open 24/7 for referrals: 1-800-662-4357.

What counts as an off day

An off day is a day with no THC in any form. Flower, a cart, a gummy, a drink, a shared hit at a friend’s place: all of them count. The rule is simple on purpose. “Just a little less” turns into a nightly negotiation, and a yes-or-no question is much easier to answer honestly at 11 pm. A few things to settle before day one:

  • CBD-only products. Decide now whether they’re allowed on off days. If you allow them, stick to one product and read its label for THC rather than grabbing whatever is on the shelf.
  • Where the day ends. Most people use in the evening, so treat the off day as the night that follows it. A joint at 1 am after an off day counts as a detour, not an early start on tomorrow.
  • What stays in the house. You don’t have to throw anything out. Putting it somewhere that takes effort to reach, like a locked box or a high shelf in another room, removes the version of you who reaches without deciding to.

Mogul days are yours to use as you normally would. If you want to go further, the same review suggests that stepping down slowly, or moving to lower-THC products over a period of weeks, may make withdrawal less likely and less intense. A lower strength on mogul days as the phases move on is an option, not a requirement.

The hard part is the night

For a lot of daily users, weed is how the day ends. It is the thing that turns the brain off before bed. So the effort of an early off day tends to land late: lying awake, a restless first few hours, waking at 3 am. When sleep does come, the dreams can be long, vivid and strange, and people who have used every night for years are often surprised by how much they remember in the morning.

That lines up with the research. The clinical review lists disturbed sleep and dreaming among the most common withdrawal features, along with irritability, anxiety, low mood and a loss of appetite. Symptoms usually start a day or two after stopping and peak somewhere between the second and sixth day, and sleep trouble can outlast the rest by several weeks. In the first phase of this plan an off day is a single night, so what you notice will mostly be the front edge of that. The run of off days gets longer in later phases, and that is where the week-one pattern shows up more fully.

A few things help with off-day nights:

  • Protect a wind-down. Give the hour before bed a routine that isn’t a screen: a shower, a book, stretching, the same playlist. It replaces the signal the joint used to send.
  • Expect to be awake for a while. Going to bed at the usual time and lying there for an hour is normal early on. Getting up for twenty minutes and trying again works better than staring at the ceiling.
  • Keep something small to eat nearby. Appetite can drop on off days. A regular meal at a regular time is easier than waiting until you feel hungry.

Irritability is the other one people mention. You might be shorter with people than you mean to be, especially on the evening of an off day. It can help to tell whoever you live with which days are off days, so a sharp tone gets read as the plan and not as a mood.

Cues and what to put in their place

Daily use is usually tied to a few repeated moments: getting home from work, gaming, a show after dinner, a particular friend, the ten minutes before sleep. The first couple of off days will tell you which of those moments pulls hardest. Plan for the loud ones:

  • Change the order. If the first hit happens the moment you walk in, cook first, shower first, or go for a short walk before you sit down.
  • Keep your hands busy. Rolling and packing are part of the ritual. A cup of tea you have to make, a puzzle, a guitar, or a game that needs both hands covers that stretch.
  • Check the calendar a week ahead. The calculator shows which days are off days. If a party or a night with the friends you smoke with lands on one, decide now how you’ll handle it, or swap the plans to a mogul day.
  • Ride out the wave. Cravings come in bursts and pass. The Slope app’s Ride It Out exercise walks you through the few minutes it takes.

Detours

Smoking on an off day is a detour. Log it and keep the rest of the calendar as it was. There’s no need to add extra off days to make up for it, and no reason to write off the week. Over a few weeks the log usually points at something specific: the same weekday, the same people, the nights after bad sleep. That is the useful part. If you detour more than twice in one phase, stay there longer using the app’s extend option instead of stepping up.

If you keep getting stuck in the same place, it’s worth knowing that this is common, not a personal flaw. The CDC estimates that about 3 in 10 people who use cannabis have cannabis use disorder, meaning they can’t stop even when it is causing problems, and that the risk is higher for people who use more often. If that sounds like you, a clinician or counselor can help, and you can keep using a schedule alongside that support.

Who this is for

Adults who use cannabis most days, legally where they live, and want more THC-free days without committing today to quitting for good. It works just as well as a first step toward stopping entirely. If you are pregnant, use other substances, or live with anxiety, depression or another condition you’ve been managing with weed, talk to a clinician about the plan.
