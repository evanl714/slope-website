Habit taper calculator / Caffeine

Plan a gradual caffeine taper.

Coffee, tea, energy drinks, pre-workout: pick a start ratio and a finish date, and Slope maps a daily schedule with a little more space between caffeine days.

Quitting caffeine all at once is famous for one thing: the headache. Withdrawal symptoms usually start 12 to 24 hours after your last dose, peak somewhere in the first two days, and can hang around for up to a week or so. Spreading the change over a few weeks is the standard way to avoid most of that, and this calculator builds that schedule for you.

The calculator starts you at a 1:3 ratio over 60 days, which means one caffeine-free day for every three regular days, stepping up to five caffeine-free days in a row by the end. Change either setting to fit your own routine.

How to use this schedule for caffeine

Slope schedules days, not milligrams, so the first step is deciding what an off day means for you. Pick one definition before day one and keep it for the whole plan:

  • Decaf day. Swap every caffeinated drink for decaf or herbal. Decaf coffee still carries a few milligrams, which is part of why it works as a soft landing.
  • Half day. Caffeine before noon only. This suits people who mostly want to fix their sleep and don’t need to reach zero.
  • Zero day. No caffeine at all, including chocolate and soda. The cleanest definition and the one most likely to trigger a headache early on, so pair it with a gentler start ratio like 1:3.

Mogul days are your regular caffeine days. They don’t have to stay regular, though. The combination that works best for most people is spacing plus shrinking: keep the schedule above, and as the phases progress, make each mogul day a little smaller too. A large coffee becomes a medium, then a half-caf. By the last phase, a mogul day might be a single cup of black tea.

Where the caffeine actually is

Tapering is easier when you know what you’re counting. Typical amounts per serving, rounded, from published nutrition data:

DrinkServingCaffeine
Brewed coffee8 ozabout 95 mg
Espresso1 shotabout 65 mg
Energy drink8 oz70 to 100 mg
Black tea8 ozabout 45 mg
Green tea8 ozabout 30 mg
Cola12 ozabout 35 mg
Decaf coffee8 oz2 to 5 mg

A 16-ounce shop latte is two shots, so around 130 mg, and a 20-ounce drip coffee can be over 200 mg. If your day starts at 400 mg, the current guideline ceiling for healthy adults, even a 1:3 start with slightly smaller mogul days is a real change.

What to expect along the way

Early off days. Expect a dull headache, some fog, and low energy on your first few off days, especially if you chose zero days. Water, a walk, and an earlier night usually cover it. Over-the-counter pain relievers help, but check the label: some “migraine” formulas contain caffeine and will quietly undo the day.

Middle phases. Once you’re at 2:1 or 3:1, the off days stop being an event. This is where sleep tends to improve, and where the urge changes from “I need coffee” to “I miss the ritual.” Keep the ritual and change the cup.

Detours. A coffee on an off day is a detour, not a reset. Log it, keep the next mogul day where it is, and if you detour more than twice in one phase, give that phase more time in the app instead of pushing on to the next ratio.

Is it safe to taper caffeine on your own?

For most healthy adults, yes. Caffeine withdrawal is unpleasant but not medically dangerous. Talk to a clinician first if you’re pregnant, take medication that interacts with caffeine, have a heart or anxiety condition, or use caffeine to manage a diagnosed condition like migraine or ADHD.
