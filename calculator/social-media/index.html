Habit taper calculator / Social media

Plan a gradual social media taper.

Screen-time limits get dismissed with one tap. A feed-free day is harder to argue with. Slope schedules those days and spaces them a little further apart each phase.

The calculator starts you at a 2:1 ratio over 30 days: two feed-free days, then one day where you scroll as usual, stepping up to five feed-free days in a row by the end. Thirty days is enough to find out what you actually miss. Stretch it to 60 or 90 if the habit runs deep.

How to use this schedule for social media

“Less social media” fails because it has no edges. Twenty minutes becomes forty with one more scroll, and daily time limits are designed to be snoozed. Slope gives the habit an edge: whole days. Decide what an off day means before you start, and be specific about which apps count.

  • No feeds. The most common definition. Messaging stays, anything with an infinite scroll goes. Instagram, TikTok, X, Reddit, YouTube Shorts, and whatever else you open without deciding to.
  • Apps off the phone. Delete the apps on off days and reinstall on mogul days. It sounds tedious, and the tedium is the mechanism. Logging back in is a decision. Tapping an icon isn’t.
  • Desktop only. Feeds are allowed, but only on a laptop, sitting down. This keeps the parts you value, like a group or a creator you follow, and removes the reflex.

Mogul days are regular days. Use them, and notice how they feel by the third phase. A lot of people find that a full day of the feed after two days away is more tiring than they expected. That’s worth knowing before you decide what your completion date actually means.

What the first off days are like

You will reach for your phone. Not occasionally; dozens of times. Each reach is a cue firing with nothing to land on, and it fades fast once the app isn’t there. The first two or three off days feel long and a little empty, especially in the gaps: waiting in line, the bathroom, the minute before sleep. Put something in the gaps on purpose. A book on the phone, a podcast queued, a note to write.

By the 3:1 phase most people report the opposite problem: the mogul day feels like a chore they scheduled, and they get through it faster. That’s the taper working. The habit hasn’t been fought; it’s been given less room.

Set up the phone to match the calendar

Willpower is the wrong tool for a phone that was built by people who study willpower. Make the off days structural:

  • Use Screen Time or Focus modes to block the feed apps on off days. Set the downtime schedule to match your calendar from the calculator.
  • Move the apps off the home screen on every day, so opening one is always a search, not a tap.
  • Turn off notifications from every feed app permanently. Notifications are the feed reaching out to you on your off days.
  • Tell one person. Off days are easier when someone else knows which days they are.

Detours

Opening a feed on an off day is a detour. Close it, log it, keep going. A detour that lasts thirty seconds is a completely different thing from one that lasts an hour, and the log will show you which kind you tend to have. If you detour more than twice in a phase, give that phase more time in the app rather than moving on. If the whole plan keeps collapsing on the same day of the week, look at what that day has in it. It’s usually boredom or a specific relationship, and both are fixable outside the app.

Who this is for

Anyone who wants a structured way to spend less time in feeds without a dramatic, all-or-nothing detox they’ll abandon in a week. If scrolling is tied to anxiety, depression, or compulsive behavior that’s affecting your life in bigger ways, a therapist is a better first stop. Slope is a habit-planning tool, not treatment.
